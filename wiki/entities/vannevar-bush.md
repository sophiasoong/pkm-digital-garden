## Overview

Vannevar Bush (1890–1974) was an American engineer and science administrator. During World War II he directed the US Office of Scientific Research and Development. In July 1945 he published "As We May Think" in *The Atlantic*, describing the **Memex** — a hypothetical personal knowledge device that would let individuals store, search, and link their accumulated documents via associative trails.

Bush's Memex article is one of the most cited documents in computing history. It directly inspired the development of hypertext, personal computing, and the World Wide Web — and, decades later, Karpathy explicitly invokes it as the intellectual ancestor of the LLM Wiki.

## Key Facts

- Director of the US Office of Scientific Research and Development during WWII. [[sources/antigravity-karpathy-llm-wiki-2026]]
- Published "As We May Think" in *The Atlantic*, July 1945. [[sources/antigravity-karpathy-llm-wiki-2026]]
- Described the Memex: a desk-sized device for storing and linking personal knowledge via associative trails. [[sources/antigravity-karpathy-llm-wiki-2026]]
- Quote: "Wholly new forms of encyclopedias will appear, ready-made with a mesh of associative trails running through them." [[sources/antigravity-karpathy-llm-wiki-2026]]
- His article directly inspired Douglas Engelbart (personal computing), Ted Nelson (hypertext), and Tim Berners-Lee (the web). [[sources/antigravity-karpathy-llm-wiki-2026]]
