## [2026-10-06] ingest | Quo Vadis, Mathematics? (Christian Szegedy)
- Source: [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]
- New pages: [[21 Entities/christian-szegedy]], [[22 Concepts/ai-in-mathematics]], [[22 Concepts/status-economy-of-expertise]], [[22 Concepts/static-thinking-fallacy]]
- Updated pages: [[22 Concepts/voluntary-discomfort]], [[22 Concepts/stoicism]], [[_index]], and the clip file in `10 Inbox & Raw/Web Clips/` (`status:` + `wiki_source:`)
- Notes: Opens a third domain. The wiki held a design cluster and a philosophy cluster; this is mathematics and AI, and it arrives with no neighbors. Built three concepts rather than one on the human's call, chosen for portability: the airplane forecast, the status economy, and the named fallacy. The last two are deliberately written in general terms so later sources from other domains can link to them — flagged on each page that the wiki currently holds exactly one instance.
- The find of this ingest, and the reason it was worth three concept pages: the essay's real subject is not whether machines can do mathematics but what a field rewards. Its model of pure mathematics ranks heroic ascent of a hard problem above connecting distant regions, above tools, above applications — and states that what is admired is the heroic effort rather than the map, with the problems themselves described as mostly uninteresting testing grounds for methods. A reward structure keyed to difficulty is exactly what a tool that removes difficulty devalues, whether or not the tool is any good at the rest of the job. That is the separable claim: output and status economy can come apart.
- Cross-domain link written, as authorized — the wiki's third. [[22 Concepts/voluntary-discomfort]] names practicing discomfort for status as one of two ways the Stoic practice gets distorted. This source describes that distortion as an institution: a living professional culture distributing careers on the basis of chosen difficulty, reported without censure. Three things it makes visible that the Stoic source does not address — scale (every Stoic example is individual and private), identity as a dependency *created* by the practice rather than reduced by it (Szegedy's grief at eighteen, when nobody coached him anymore and he did not know who he was without something to train for), and hardship abolished from outside rather than chosen or imposed, which the Freedom Dimension's logic does not cover. Labelled a reading throughout; neither source mentions the other. Summarized on [[22 Concepts/stoicism]].
- Written with the author's position stated rather than assumed. He uses "we" for the side that settled the conjectures and planted the flags, and closes anticipating the view from his own supersonic airplane. That self-disclosure is to the essay's credit and is recorded on [[21 Entities/christian-szegedy]] and [[22 Concepts/ai-in-mathematics]]. No outside biography was imported — the entity page is grounded only in the essay, per the Blake precedent, and says so. This matters more than usual: his institutional position is the first thing a reader would want when weighing the forecast, and the wiki does not have it.
- Recorded as a limit, not glossed: the load-bearing empirical claim — that AI systems have settled previously open conjectures — names no conjecture, no system, no lab and no result. Stage 2 of the forecast is unverifiable from this wiki's material. Also wrote a critical section on [[22 Concepts/static-thinking-fallacy]]: the charge is real, and as stated it has no criterion for separating a limitation that will dissolve from one that will not, which makes the forecast unfalsifiable and the rebuttal universal. The mirror error — assuming an observed rate continues — is not named anywhere in the essay.
- Entity restraint, continued: Balázs Szegedy, Jared Duker Lichtman, Michael R. Douglas, the Langlands program and the IMO are all single mentions with no substance in this source, recorded in the source page's Entities Mentioned rather than given pages.
- One quote only, six words, and the reason is on the page: the essay's force is in two analogies that run for paragraphs, and reproducing them would mean reproducing the piece.
- Worth revisiting rather than leaving to stand: the forecast commits to a months-scale timeline and is therefore checkable, which is unusual. Noted as an open question on [[22 Concepts/static-thinking-fallacy]].

## [2026-10-06] ingest | What Is Liquid Glass? Apple's Design Language, Explained
- Source: [[23 Sources/liquid-glass-explained-2026]]
- New pages: [[21 Entities/apple]], [[22 Concepts/liquid-glass]]
- Updated pages: [[22 Concepts/usability]], [[22 Concepts/color-stimulation]], [[22 Concepts/hierarchy-utility-style]], [[21 Entities/material-design]], [[_index]], and the clip file in `10 Inbox & Raw/Web Clips/` (`status:` + `wiki_source:`)
- Notes: Both clips in this session captured cleanly — full article bodies, no Clipper failure and no repair needed, the first time that has happened since the two client-rendered Google sites defeated it.
- The tension in this wiki's design cluster is reframed, as authorized, and it is now a stronger statement than before. Two independent instances, different companies, different materials, same shape: Material Design's own research found the single measure M2 beat M3 on was playfulness, attributed to M2's colorful app bar; Apple drew sustained legibility criticism through the Liquid Glass betas and used the 26.1 releases to add a more opaque tinted appearance alongside the default clear one. What that jointly establishes is narrower than "expressive design harms usability" and more useful than a disagreement — an expressive visual default has a cost, the cost falls unevenly across users, and in both documented cases the vendor who shipped it paid for it in public. Google paid in a research finding its own guidance does not carry forward; Apple paid in an engineering retreat. Explicitly not established, and said so on the page: that restraint is optimal, that the costs outweigh what expressiveness buys, or that either vendor was wrong — Apple kept clear glass as the default and made opacity the option. The debate is better specified, not closed.
- Second find, and the sharper one: [[22 Concepts/color-stimulation]] holds Duru's "atmosphere" technique — low light–dark contrast plus gradients and fuzzy boundaries — as the way to bring stimulation *down*. That is a precise description of what a translucent surface does to everything behind it. Apple shipped that lever across six operating systems and the named failure mode was text on glass over a busy background. The two sources agree on the mechanism while pointing opposite ways, and together they supply something the model as stated lacks: a *functional* floor. Duru's framework has a ceiling (overstimulation reads as irritation) and an emotional floor (understimulation reads as apathy) but no vocabulary for illegible. Flagged on the page that reading the Apple case as a test of the stimulation model would be overreach — Liquid Glass is a material, not a palette, and its source discusses color only to say it is not a color scheme.
- [[21 Entities/apple]] created as the wiki's second vendor design language, which is what makes the comparison to [[21 Entities/material-design]] possible at all. The comparison is recorded as structural rather than as a disagreement: both pursued expressive visual systems and both hit legibility, but Google's reckoning arrives as a published research instrument with stated limits and Apple's as a settings toggle in a point release. Arguably the stronger evidence, since it cost something to ship, and certainly the less analysable — no study, sample or audit appears anywhere in the source.
- Provenance caveat recorded on both the source and concept pages, unprompted, because it bears on how much weight they can carry: this is not Apple documentation. The site is a reference gallery for the aesthetic and states that every gallery item carries a ready-to-use AI style prompt — a commercially interested party in the popularity of the look it describes. That interest cuts *against* its own critical passages, which is a reason to credit the legibility history; but the datable facts (versions, dates, WWDC session titles, the Malewicz attribution) are uncited and should be confirmed against Apple's own material before anything is built on them.
- Noted as an acute case of an existing problem: [[22 Concepts/color-relativity]] holds that a color has no fixed appearance because the surround changes it. A glass surface has no fixed appearance at all, by construction — so every difficulty relativity raises for fixed color tokens is sharper here, and a material whose appearance is determined by its backdrop cannot be contrast-checked before the backdrop exists. Neither source connects them.
- Entity restraint: Michal Malewicz, credited with coining "glassmorphism" in November 2020, got no page — one dateable fact with nothing attached, below the threshold already applied to the nine figures held as mentions from [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]]. The publishing site got no page either, since nothing is known of it beyond its own pages.
- No quotes taken. The passages worth keeping are the three design principles and the glassmorphism distinction, both compressed restatements rather than quotable formulations — and a secondhand explainer is the wrong document to quote as authority on Apple's material. Same treatment as [[23 Sources/duru-halfway-color-combinations-2021]].
