## Overview

Lucius Annaeus Seneca (c. 4 BCE–65 CE), known as Seneca the Younger, was a Roman Stoic philosopher, statesman, and playwright. He served as an advisor to Emperor Nero and accumulated considerable wealth and political influence — a fact he was aware created a particular obligation to practice what he preached. His most sustained philosophical work is the *Epistulae Morales ad Lucilium* (Letters to Lucilius), a collection of 124 letters on Stoic ethics and practice written near the end of his life.

He was forced to commit suicide by Nero in 65 CE after being accused of involvement in an assassination conspiracy.
