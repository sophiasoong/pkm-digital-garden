## Overview

Visual designer working on color, emotion and user experience, and co-creator of the Color Moods tool. She is the only figure in this wiki represented by two sources at different stages of her thinking: an exploratory Medium article on two-color pairing from 2021 and a systematic framework published on Google Design in 2026. The later piece names her two-color-combination work as the origin of Color Moods, which makes the arc visible — descriptive catalog, then tool, then general method.

Her distinguishing position is a refusal to separate rational color analysis from emotional response. She supplies a calculable-sounding model (stimulation as the sum of intensity, hue distance and light-dark contrast) and then explicitly subordinates it to the designer's immediate gut feeling, going so far as to rank nostalgia, bittersweetness and sadness above joy as indicators that a palette is working. She is also a critic of the field she writes in: her central polemic is that designers treat color as a binary of maximal-intensity "colorful" versus safe neutrals, and so skip the muted middle of the intensity range where most usable and most natural color lives.

## Key Facts

- Co-creator of [[21 Entities/color-moods]] (colormoods.co), presented as the operational implementation of her stimulation model [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]].
- Works with Google designers on color, emotion and UX [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]].
- Author of "Halfway Color Combinations," published on Medium (Design Bootcamp) 2021-06-15 [[23 Sources/duru-halfway-color-combinations-2021]].
- Author of "Rethinking Color Theory," published on Google Design; publication date not stated on the page [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]].
- Draws on Josef Albers for color relativity, Johannes Itten and Riccardo Falcinelli for color fundamentals, and Ingrid Fetell Lee for the stimulation framing [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]].
- Cites Susan Cain's *Bittersweet* and Christopher Alexander's *The Nature of Order* in support of ranking melancholy feeling above joy as a success signal [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]].
- Uses the **Munsell** color wheel rather than the RYB artist's wheel [[23 Sources/duru-halfway-color-combinations-2021]].
