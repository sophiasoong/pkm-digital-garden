## Overview

Mathematician at the University of California, Los Angeles; the speaker in [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]. Unlike this wiki's other figures known through a single source, he arrives with biography attached — but the biography comes from the publisher's blurb rather than from his own words, and this page keeps that distinction, because the blurb is promotional in register and the interview is not.

What he contributes to this wiki is the first voice on AI and mathematics that is neither forecasting nor grieving. He works with these systems, reports specific dated capability changes and specific unknowns with equal care, and refuses the framing both of this wiki's other sources on the subject rely on — that there is a single difficulty axis along which humans and machines can be ranked. His alternative, breadth against depth, reorganizes the question rather than answering it, and he is explicit that he does not know how replicable the recent results are.

He is also, incidentally, the wiki's best source on how mathematical work actually feels from inside: he says he has never had a eureka moment, would like one, and gets instead a long process of productive failure ending in the feeling that he should have seen it earlier.

## Key Facts

- Professor of mathematics at UCLA. Has a forthcoming book organized around six mathematical concepts, which supplies the interview's structure. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Has followed AI developments in mathematics closely for about three years at the time of the interview, and describes a steady progression rather than a jump. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Reports using AI tools mainly for secondary tasks — literature search, checking a proof, writing code, proofreading for tightness — and not for the problem-solving process itself, attributing this to rhythm and the inability to attune to a model as to a long-term collaborator. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Says he has stopped trying to stay current with all developments in his own field, and can no longer promise to read every one. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Says he has never had a eureka moment and would love for one to happen; his process is iterated failure, mapping the negative space of techniques that fail until the path appears almost by elimination. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Credits collaboration for much of what he knows, learned after graduate school by trading knowledge with mathematicians and scientists in other fields — and says he did not realize how important this was until relatively late in his career. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Names compressed sensing, which came out of meeting a statistician and an electrical engineer at a Los Angeles mathematics institute working on slow MRI scans, as the application of his own work he is most proud of. It is now taught beside least squares. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Lives in Los Angeles and uses its traffic as his standing example of emergent dynamics. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
- Biography from the publisher's blurb, not his own words, and recorded as such: born in Adelaide, Australia; working at university-level mathematics by age nine; at thirteen the youngest-ever gold medalist at the International Mathematical Olympiad; PhD from Princeton at twenty; UCLA's youngest-ever tenured professor at twenty-four; Fields Medal in 2006 for work spanning partial differential equations, combinatorics, harmonic analysis and additive number theory; known for the Green–Tao theorem on arithmetic progressions in the primes. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]]
