## Overview

Author of [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]], an essay arguing that pure mathematics is about to be industrialized by AI. Everything on this page is drawn from that essay and nothing else — no outside biography has been imported, per the thin-stub practice this wiki applies to figures known only through a single source. That restraint matters more than usual here, because the author's institutional position is the thing a reader would most want to weigh when assessing the forecast, and the essay discloses it only obliquely.

What the essay does establish is a double standing. Szegedy was formed by Hungarian competition mathematics and writes about mathematicians with the familiarity of someone who was one, and he writes about AI from inside the effort — using "we" for the side that has been settling conjectures and planting flags on open problems. The essay is simultaneously an analysis of a transition and a statement from one party to it, which it acknowledges rather than conceals.

## Key Facts

- Attended a mathematics-specialized class at what he describes as the most prestigious school in Hungary: eight hours of mathematics a week plus separate sessions preparing for national and international competitions. [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]
- Met his ex-wife at a week-long camp at Lake Balaton whose only activity was working on International Mathematical Olympiad problems. [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]
- Never came close to the Hungarian IMO team, but describes belonging to the community around it as one of the most important parts of his life. [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]
- Reports the two hours after his high school finals as the freest of his life, followed by days of sadness at the realization that the competitions were over — nobody coached him anymore and nobody cared. He uses this as the template for the grief he predicts mathematicians will feel. [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]
- Has a brother, Balázs, named as a mathematician who embraced the AI changes early. [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]
- Writes from the AI-development side of the transition he describes, using "we" for the aviation companies in his own analogy and closing in anticipation of the view from his own supersonic airplane. [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]
- Names Jared Duker Lichtman and Michael R. Douglas as mathematicians who have been outspoken in working toward the same vision. [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]

## Contradictions

No source contradicts the biography — he remains the only source about himself, and nothing outside the essay has been imported. What has changed is that the forecast is now contested on substance by two later sources, neither of which mentions him by name. Recorded in full on [[22 Concepts/ai-in-mathematics]]; the short form:

- **Stage 4 of the forecast is directly contradicted.** Cheap charter flights require costs that fall. [[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]] reports that the cost of an attempt is undisclosed because the instruments are privately owned, the success rate is unknown, and perhaps only around 1% of problems are amenable at all. Nothing in this wiki supports the cheapness.
- **His axis is rejected outright by his structural twin.** [[21 Entities/ken-ono]] occupies the same position — a mathematician employed on the AI side, reporting the change first-hand — and says the race should be for more truth rather than more compute, which makes verifiability the goal rather than throughput. The two also choose analogies that point in opposite directions from identical standing: Wright brothers against sharecropper and tractor.
- **The single difficulty axis is called the error.** [[21 Entities/terence-tao]] argues humans and machines occupy different regions of a two-dimensional space rather than different points on one line, which undercuts the trajectory argument without claiming the systems will stop improving.
- **The uncited claim is partly rescued, not verified.** Both later sources name the Erdős unit distance conjecture, and Ono attributes it to OpenAI. That is corroboration of a nearby claim by other interested parties. Stage 2 moves from unverifiable to plausible.

The standing caveat holds: the essay's central empirical claim is made by someone on the side making it, with no conjecture, system or result named. Whatever his wider position in the field is, this wiki still does not know it, and a source *about* him rather than *by* him would change how the forecast should be read.
