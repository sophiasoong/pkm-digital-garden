## Overview

Marcus Porcius Cato Uticensis, known as Cato the Younger (95–46 BCE), was a Roman senator, Stoic, and one of Julius Caesar's principal political opponents. He is remembered for uncompromising moral integrity — refusing to negotiate, accept Caesar's mercy, or participate in what he saw as the corruption of the Roman Republic. He died by suicide at Utica rather than surrender to Caesar.

Plutarch's life of Cato records his deliberate physical training: he went outdoors in all seasons with his head uncovered, walked everywhere on foot, and sometimes went through the streets without shoes or a tunic. He was not performing for public approval — Plutarch explicitly notes this — but training himself to distinguish genuine shame from the discomfort of being judged.
