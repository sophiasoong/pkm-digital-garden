## Overview

An AI company working on mathematics, named by [[21 Entities/ken-ono]] as his employer in [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]. This page is deliberately thin, because the source is thin: Ono speaks about the company's work with real specificity in one place — the formalization of mathematical economics — and otherwise mentions it as context for his own position. No outside material has been imported, and nothing here is corroborated.

What the wiki can say is that Axiom is the institutional home of the one concrete formalization result it holds, and that its existence is part of the argument Ono makes rather than background to it: his answer to AI displacing mathematicians is a programme his own company runs, which is worth noting when weighing the recommendation that students should start formalizing.

## Key Facts

- Ono's employer, alongside the University of Virginia. [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]
- Does work Ono describes under the heading of formalization: translating natural language into verifiable computer code, then having AI examine that code for vulnerabilities. [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]
- Is partnering with a Harvard mathematical economist on formalizing mathematical theories in economics. The transcript renders the name as "Scott Coming," apparently a speech-to-text corruption; this wiki does not guess at the intended name. [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]
- That work reportedly found that some foundational theorems of the subject were not accurately portrayed, implemented or applied — the stated example being Robert Aumann's theorem on whether parties with common priors can agree to disagree, whose fiftieth anniversary falls in 2026. [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]
- Ono describes this as a viral moment in mathematical economics, with economists from around the world joining the effort. [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]
- The effort is described as extending to computer scientists formalizing machine learning itself. [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]
- Ono's framing of what the company does with AI: it supports mathematical work in a number of ways and lowers the burden of performing discovery, rather than performing discovery itself. [[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]]
