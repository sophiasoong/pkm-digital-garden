## Overview

Lao Tzu is the philosopher behind the Tao Te Ching, presented here through Ursula K. Le Guin's rendering and commentary. This wiki's only source on him so far is a single paragraph (her Chapter 80 commentary), so this page is necessarily a stub — it reflects one specific teaching, not a biography or overview of his broader philosophy.

## Key Facts

- Rejects both materialistic dualism (ignoring the body, living in the head) and religious dualism (despising the body for a heavenly reward) as "dangerous and in danger." [[23 Sources/le-guin-tao-te-ching-1997]]
- Alternative offered, per Le Guin's rendering: "live in your body, you are your body; where else is there to go?" [[23 Sources/le-guin-tao-te-ching-1997]]
- Treats heaven and earth as one — ordinary daily life (walking the streets of your town) is itself "the Way of heaven." [[23 Sources/le-guin-tao-te-ching-1997]]
