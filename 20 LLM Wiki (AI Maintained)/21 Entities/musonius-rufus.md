## Overview

Gaius Musonius Rufus (c. 30–100 CE) was a Roman Stoic philosopher, sometimes called "the Roman Socrates." He taught in Rome during the reigns of Nero and Vespasian and is known for treating philosophy not as an academic discipline but as a form of practical training applied to daily life. His most famous student was Epictetus. His lectures were recorded by his students and survive in fragments.

His central pedagogical conviction was that philosophical practice must include the body as well as the mind — that virtue is cultivated through habituation and exercise, not merely through understanding.

## Key Facts

- Roman Stoic philosopher, approximately 30–100 CE. [[23 Sources/stoic-wisdoms-voluntary-discomfort-2026]]
- Teacher of Epictetus. [[23 Sources/stoic-wisdoms-voluntary-discomfort-2026]]
- Argued that a person who avoids all hardship becomes less prepared to endure it. [[23 Sources/stoic-wisdoms-voluntary-discomfort-2026]]
- Taught that philosophical practice should include becoming accustomed to cold, heat, thirst, hunger, limited food, hard beds, and physical strain. [[23 Sources/stoic-wisdoms-voluntary-discomfort-2026]]
- His point: endurance and self-control must be practised *before* circumstances demand them — they cannot appear for the first time in an emergency. [[23 Sources/stoic-wisdoms-voluntary-discomfort-2026]]
