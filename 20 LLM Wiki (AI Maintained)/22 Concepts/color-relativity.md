## Definition

Color relativity is the principle that a color has no fixed appearance: what it looks like depends on what surrounds it. The visual system does not report colors neutrally but actively exaggerates differences, pushing neighboring hues, lightnesses and intensities apart from one another. The effect strengthens when one color surrounds another rather than merely abutting it [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]].

The practical corollary, and the reason the concept matters for design rather than only for perception research, is that analyzing a color on its own is close to useless. A swatch judged in isolation will not behave the same way in context. The source carries this to its strongest form in the single line this wiki quotes from it: there is no such thing as a perfectly neutral backdrop — grays, whites and blacks are participants in the relationship, not inert stages for it.

The lineage is explicit. [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]] credits Josef Albers's *Interaction of Color* as "the original color relativity manual," making Duru a transmitter and applier of the idea rather than its originator.

## How It Appears in This Wiki

Relativity appears in one source, where it functions as groundwork rather than as the topic. [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]] establishes it before introducing its method, because the method's unit of analysis is the combination. Two illusions are used as demonstration: a single continuous strip of color reads as two different colors depending only on what sits behind each end.

Its structural role in the wiki is to explain why [[22 Concepts/color-stimulation]] is specified the way it is. Hue *distance*, light–dark *contrast*, overall intensity of the *combination* — every term is relational, and relativity is the reason. A model built on absolute color values would be measuring something the eye does not see.

The concept also cuts against the way color is usually handled in design systems, which specify colors as tokens with fixed values. Nothing in the ingested sources makes that argument, and it is not attributed here — but it is the obvious next question, recorded below.

## Open Questions

- If no backdrop is neutral and context changes appearance, how should a design system express color? Fixed tokens encode exactly the context-independence this concept denies.
- How large is the effect in practice at interface scale and typical viewing conditions? The demonstrations are deliberately constructed illusions.
- Is relativity ever usable as a tactic rather than a hazard — choosing a surround specifically to shift a color's apparent hue? The source treats it as something to account for, not to exploit.
- Albers is credited but not ingested. The wiki holds the idea secondhand.
