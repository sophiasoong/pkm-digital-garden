## Definition

Halfway combinations are color pairs whose hues sit roughly a quarter-turn — about 90° — apart on the color wheel. They occupy the gap between the two conventional recipes: analogous pairs, which sit close together and risk blandness, and complementary pairs, which sit opposite and are the textbook move. The claim is that the halfway interval is far enough apart to hold tension while still sharing enough to cohere [[23 Sources/duru-halfway-color-combinations-2021]].

The concept is defined by catalogue rather than by principle. Nine pairs are named and characterized by the mood each produces: red/purple, orange/magenta, yellow/magenta, yellow/red, green/orange, green/yellow, blue/green, purple/blue-green, blue/magenta. Which pairs qualify depends on which wheel is used — the source works from the **Munsell** wheel, not the RYB artist's wheel, and the two disagree about what lies a quarter-turn away.

## How It Appears in This Wiki

This is the earlier of two [[21 Entities/ruxandra-duru]] sources and the narrower idea. It appears as exploration: careful description of what each pair feels like, with no account of why the interval works.

Its significance in this wiki is less as a technique than as the origin point of a documented five-year arc. The source's own conclusion undercuts its premise — value and saturation choices alter a pair's mood more than the choice of hue pair does — and that concession is the hinge. [[23 Sources/duru-rethinking-color-theory-2026]] builds exactly there, demoting hue distance to one of three properties alongside intensity and light–dark contrast, and names Duru's two-color-combination work as the origin of [[21 Entities/color-moods]]. So the wiki holds a rare thing: an idea, the author's own recognition of its limits, and the framework that superseded it, all from the same hand.

Read forward, the concept is best understood as a **heuristic for hue distance** — a specific setting of one slider on the later model's mixing board, discovered before the board existed.

## Tensions & Debates

**The source argues against itself.** The article is organized around hue pairing and concludes that hue pairing is the weaker variable. Taken seriously, this makes the nine-pair catalogue an entry point rather than a principle — useful for getting started, not for explaining results. The author's later work treats it this way, which is the strongest available reading of the concession.

**Wheel dependence.** Munsell and RYB disagree about what sits 90° away, so the same prescription yields different pairs depending on an unstated choice. The source names Munsell but does not defend the choice or note that it changes the answer.

## Open Questions

- Is there a perceptual basis for 90°, or is it a convenient interval that avoids both blandness and the complementary cliché?
- Are the nine mood characterizations culturally portable, or one designer's associations? They are presented as observations without qualification.
- Does anything survive of the halfway idea once value and saturation are acknowledged as dominant — or is it fully absorbed by [[22 Concepts/color-stimulation]]?
