## Definition

A field's status economy is its internal system of recognition: which work earns reverence, which earns respect, which earns a line in a grant proposal, and therefore what practitioners spend their lives doing. The concept's useful claim is that a field's *output* and its *status economy* are separable — the first can survive while the second is inverted — and that automation acts on the second one first.

The sharpest version in this wiki's source material is the observation that what a community admires is not the result but the difficulty of obtaining it. In the jungle model of pure mathematics, the greatest recognition goes to whoever climbs a tall previously unclimbed rock and sketches a rough map from the summit, and what people admire most is explicitly stated to be the heroic effort rather than the map [[23 Sources/szegedy-quo-vadis-mathematics-2026]]. A reward structure keyed to difficulty is precisely the structure that a tool which removes difficulty devalues, regardless of whether the tool is any good at the rest of the job.

## The Ranking, as One Field Expresses It

The source's model of pure mathematics is specific enough to be worth holding as data rather than as metaphor. Highest to lowest:

1. **Heroic ascent** — settling a hard problem that earlier attempts failed at. Reverence, especially if others tried and failed.
2. **Connecting distant regions** — a result that lets work travel between two separated areas. Highly valued.
3. **Tools** — a widely used method or construction. Well known but without the halo; and nobody believes a tool is useful until it is used to climb something nobody had climbed.
4. **Applications** — something that benefits people outside the field. Earns "some respect," gets cited in grant proposals, and is otherwise of little internal interest.

Two features of this ranking matter more than the order. Tools are validated only through rock-climbing, so the second-class achievement cannot even be recognized on its own terms. And the conjectures at the top are described as mostly uninteresting in themselves — testing grounds for methods — which means the field's highest honors attach to difficulty almost in the abstract.

## How It Appears in This Wiki

Through one source, about one field, written at a moment of forecast change. The source's own thesis is that this ranking is about to be rewritten: the specific heroism of climbing what nobody else could will matter less, and choosing which terrain is worth exploring, directing fleets of instruments, and explaining results to the outside world will matter more. Academic form — universities, journals, tenure committees — is predicted to look much the same for years while what it rewards shifts underneath.

The source also names the human cost without minimizing it: many practitioners will feel disorientation and a sudden loss of purpose, and the author grounds this in his own experience of losing competition mathematics at eighteen. For people embedded in the current ranking, the change registers as a demotion, and some will fight it.

## The Second and Third Voices

Two later sources describe the same structure and reach different verdicts, which is what this page was written in general terms to accommodate.

[[23 Sources/ono-nine-lessons-2026]] is the direct counterweight. He describes a recognition system keyed to difficulty in almost the same terms and treats it as corrupting rather than as about to be devalued: benchmarks measuring human mathematical ability concentrate prestige on performance at hard problems, the scores then drive admissions, hiring and funding, and the measure stops tracking anything about mathematics. Where Szegedy's worry is that the currency is about to collapse, his is that the currency was bad to begin with — and he does not think the collapse will cost many jobs. See [[22 Concepts/benchmark-critique]].

[[23 Sources/tao-six-essentials-2026]] supplies the mechanism Szegedy's forecast leaves implicit, from inside the pipeline. Two concrete changes in how recognition gets allocated: the drop-everything attention that a rare major solution used to command is being replaced by triage, because there is now more to absorb than anyone can read; and the work that is now scarce — readable write-up, peer digestion, curation and filtering — is precisely the work the status economy does not reward. He also names what Szegedy does not, which is that the apprenticeship through which people entered the status economy at all is the part most exposed. See [[22 Concepts/research-bottleneck-shift]].

The three agree on the structure and disagree on the verdict: about to be inverted, already wrong, or shifting toward work nothing currently credits.

## Why It Is Worth a Page

This is the most portable idea in its source. The structure — a field whose honors attach to the difficulty of a task rather than the value of the result, meeting a technology that removes the difficulty — is not specific to mathematics, and this page exists to be linked to from future sources in other domains. It is stated here in general terms deliberately, while flagging that the wiki currently has exactly one instance of it.

## Tensions & Debates

**Does the output survive the inversion?** The source assumes mathematics keeps being produced, in greater volume, by people motivated differently. But if the reward for doing the hard part is what drew people in, removing it is not obviously neutral for supply. The essay forecasts more casual explorers joining — charter a plane, buy tools at the shop, climb with friends — which is a prediction about participation, not about whether anyone still does the work that the airplanes cannot do.

**Meritocracy as accelerant or as casualty.** The source names mathematics the most meritocratic academic field and treats this as the reason the transition will go through: merit is redefined and the field follows. The opposite reading is available and the source does not address it — a field that distributes status strictly by demonstrated individual difficulty has more invested in difficulty remaining scarce than a field that distributes status by institutional position.

**Single instance, general claim.** The ranking above is one person's account of one field, offered as analogy and not as survey. Whether other fields' status economies are keyed to difficulty in the same way is untested here. The two later sources add corroboration but not independence — all three are mathematicians, two of them employed on the AI side, so the wiki now has three accounts of one field rather than one account of three.

**Was it ever keyed to difficulty, or to difficulty that others witnessed?** The second source's benchmark account suggests the currency is *legible* difficulty — a score, a ranking, a problem others publicly failed at — which is not the same as difficulty itself. If so, automation's effect depends on whether the legibility survives, and nobody here addresses that.

## Open Questions

- Is the ranking recognized by mathematicians, or is it an outsider-insider's characterization? The author has standing to describe it and an interest in its devaluation.
- What happens to the second tier — connecting distant regions, building theories — which the source places out of reach of current instruments and which is also where most of a field's durable value sits?
- Does a status economy keyed to taste and direction-setting ("choosing which terrain is worth exploring") distribute recognition as legibly as one keyed to difficulty? Difficulty is at least checkable.
- Is there a field that has been through this and kept its output intact? The first source asserts other fields have survived similar upheavals without naming one.
- If recognition moves toward curation and direction-setting, what credits it? Three sources identify the shift and none names an institution that rewards the new work.
- Can a status economy be deliberately redesigned? The second source is the only one who proposes trying, and his proposal is about what to stop measuring rather than what to start.
