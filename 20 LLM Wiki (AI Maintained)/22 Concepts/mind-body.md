## Definition

The claim that mind and body aren't two separate things where one should rule the other — they're one thing. Per Le Guin's rendering of Lao Tzu: "you are your body; where else is there to go?" There's no higher self hiding behind or above the body that the body serves. The body isn't a vehicle for you — it is you.

## How It Appears in This Wiki

Introduced in [[23 Sources/le-guin-tao-te-ching-1997]] as the positive alternative to [[22 Concepts/dualism]]. Where "dualism" names the problem (splitting mind and body), "mind-body" names Lao Tzu's proposed resolution — there's no split to begin with. The passage extends this outward: "heaven and earth are one," and ordinary daily activity (walking the streets of your town) is itself participation in "the Way of heaven." The unity isn't just internal — it's cosmological too.

## Related Concepts

- [[22 Concepts/dualism]] — The split this concept says doesn't actually exist.
- [[22 Concepts/attentive-perception]] — Thematic parallel, not the same claim. This concept collapses the split between mind and body ("you are your body"); attentive perception collapses a different split — small/vast, momentary/eternal — via close attention rather than embodiment. Both refuse to treat the immediate and present as lesser than something bigger held elsewhere.

## Open Questions

- The passage asserts unity ("you are your body") but doesn't say what that changes day to day — what would count as evidence someone is living this out, versus just agreeing with it as an idea?
- How does "heaven and earth are one" relate to the personal mind-body claim — is the cosmic unity doing real philosophical work, or is it a poetic extension of the personal one?
